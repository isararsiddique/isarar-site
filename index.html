Zero Dementia

In clinical trial

Biotech Wallah · clinical trial at SGPGI Lucknow — ENT and Neurology departments

Screening for early mild cognitive impairment using nothing but a stock smartphone. No MRI, no lumbar puncture, no specialist. Four signal streams are captured in a single sitting — a spoken passage, facial video during prompted expression, fine-motor tapping, and a short cognitive battery — and fused into one risk score in under fifteen minutes.

The trial runs across two departments at SGPGI — ENT and Neurology — because the signals cut across both: hearing and speech markers sit with ENT, cognitive and motor markers with neurology. Both arms are nearing completion. The hard part was never the model. It was consent infrastructure, recruitment workflow, and getting usable signal out of rooms with bad lighting and background noise.

SuppliAi

Live

Convolity AI · convolity.com

Most supplement advice ignores the two things that matter: what else you're taking, and who you are. SuppliAi builds a single patient representation from lab results, prescriptions (via OCR), and wearable time series, then reasons over drug–supplement interaction pathways against it.

There's also a longitudinal biological-age model scoring six body systems over time, which is the part I'm least confident about and most interested in. Composite ageing scores are easy to make and hard to validate.

Healoncal

Live

Co-founder & Chief AI Officer · healoncal.com

Dermatological assessment from a phone camera. The interesting constraint here isn't accuracy in aggregate — it's accuracy evenly. Most skin models are trained on light skin and quietly degrade on darker tones, which makes them worse than useless in the markets that need them most.

My side of it is model architecture and getting lab-grade models onto ordinary phones without the accuracy collapsing on the way down.

Corneal endothelium analysis

Private Patent filed

Ophthalmology · specular microscopy

Automated cell morphometry from specular microscopy images — endothelial cell density, polymegathism (size variation) and pleomorphism (shape variation). These three numbers decide whether a cornea is viable for transplant, and they're currently counted semi-manually.

Private because it runs on restricted clinical imaging and there's a provisional patent filed in India on the approach. Happy to walk through the method over email.

How I got here

I'm 22. I finished a biotechnology degree in Lucknow in 2025 at a college with no research pipeline and no route into any lab I wanted. So I stopped applying and started sending people finished work instead — read their last three papers, find a gap or an unmined dataset, offer two weeks of analysis. That got me into eleven labs including Harvard, Stanford, ISRO and IISc. I wrote up exactly how.

The same move got Zero Dementia its hospitals: build the screening infrastructure first, then ask clinicians to tell you what's wrong with it. That's a conversation they want to have. Asking for patient access is not.

Lately I've been going down the stack into hardware — KiCad, Fusion, LTspice — because every model I build is bounded by the sensor in front of it, and I got tired of treating that as someone else's problem.
